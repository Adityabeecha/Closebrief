From closed books to a signed-off brief.

The same steps your close already has. Closebrief does the computing and the first draft; the judgement stays with you.

W/PStepWhat Closebrief doesYour part
B-1ImportCSV or Excel, any layoutDetects columns from NetSuite, QuickBooks or spreadsheet exports, long or wide, and joins budgets from a separate file.Confirm the mapping and pick the KPIs.
B-2ComputeDeterministic KPIsCalculates value, month-over-month, year-over-year, budget variance, trends, streaks and anomalies in code.Nothing. No model touches a number.
B-3DraftCommentary with the whyWrites plain-English commentary for each KPI, citing the context notes you keep: price changes, campaigns, one-offs.Add context the data can't know.
B-4CheckEvery figure, every timeMatches each number in the prose to a computed fact. Anything it can't trace is sent back once, then flagged.Look at what's flagged.
B-5ReviewPrepared, reviewed, approvedKeeps each draft marked until someone approves it, with version history and reviewer assignment.Edit, approve or request changes.
B-6ShareDigest, board pack, deckBuilds the executive digest, a print-ready board pack and a PowerPoint deck, and sends to Slack or email on a schedule.Send it.

How every figure is checked.

  1. Numbers first.Every KPI is computed from your data before any text is written.
  2. Words second.The model gets those figures, already formatted, and is told to copy them exactly.
  3. Then a check.Each number in the draft must match a computed fact or a figure in your own context notes. Rounding like $271K is allowed; invention isn't.
  4. You sign off.Drafts stay dashed until a person approves them.
