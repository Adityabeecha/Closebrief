Privacy

Last updated 14 November 2026

Closebrief is built for real month-end data. This page says plainly what it collects, where that goes, how long it's kept, and how you take it out or delete it yourself.

What's collected

  • Your account: your email address. If you sign in with Google, also Google's ID for your account (to recognise you next time). Passwords for email sign-in are handled by Supabase, not stored by Closebrief.
  • What you put in: files you import, the figures and KPIs computed from them, notes you add, commentary drafted and edited, and review decisions.
  • An activity log: who approved, edited, imported, synced or sent what, and when. It's kept so changes can be traced.
  • Usage: each AI request's size and cost, used to enforce the daily allowance.

Your workspace is private to your account and the people you invite. Closebrief is a small independent project and hasn't had an independent security audit (such as SOC 2); decide whether that's acceptable for the data you bring.

Where it goes

  • Hosting and storage: the app runs on Render and keeps its data in a Supabase Postgres database.
  • AI drafting: when you draft commentary, ask a question or paste a document to turn into notes, the KPI's computed figures, the relevant notes, the pasted text and your question are sent to the AI provider (OpenAI, through its API) to write the text. Your files themselves aren't sent. OpenAI doesn't use API data to train its models, and keeps it for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring.
  • Hiding names from the AI: in Settings → Workspace and data you can list words (your company, customers, people) that are replaced with placeholders like [Name 1] before anything is sent, and put back in what comes back.
  • Error reports: if error monitoring (Sentry) is switched on, crash reports may include the page or request where the error happened.
  • Linked sources: if you connect a Google Sheet or CSV link, Closebrief fetches that link to sync it.

Nothing is sold or shared for advertising. There are no ads or tracking scripts.

Cookies and browser storage

  • cb_session: keeps you signed in for up to 12 hours. Scripts on the page can't read it.
  • cb_signed_in: just tells the home page you're signed in. It holds no account details.
  • Preferences in browser storage: theme, sidebar, the dataset and workspace you last opened, and dismissed tips.

How long it's kept

  • Uploaded files are deleted 7 days after upload. The figures imported from them stay with your dataset.
  • Your datasets, notes and commentary are kept until you delete them.
  • A workspace unused for 12 months is deleted along with its datasets, notes, commentary and linked sources.
  • An import you started but didn't finish is deleted after two days.
  • The activity log and your sign-in record are kept after a workspace is deleted, so the history can't quietly disappear.

Removing your data

In Settings → Workspace and data you can download everything in your workspace as one file, and delete all of it in one step. You can also delete single datasets from the dataset menu. To have your account itself removed as well, open a request on the project's GitHub issues from an account that lets you be identified.
